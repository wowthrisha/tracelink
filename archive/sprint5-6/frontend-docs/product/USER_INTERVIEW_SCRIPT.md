# User Interview Script — SecureDoc Beta

**Date:** 2026-06-23  
**Sprint:** 5.1 — Customer Validation System  
**Purpose:** Structured guide for 45-minute discovery + product evaluation interviews with beta users.  
**Format:** 1:1, video call or in-person. Interviewer takes notes; do not rely on memory.  
**Rule:** You are learning, not selling. Never defend the product. Never correct a wrong assumption. Let the user be wrong.

---

## Before the Interview

**Prepare:**
- [ ] Confirm persona category (Architect / Consultant / Professional Services / Founder)
- [ ] Review their LinkedIn or website — know their firm size, what they do, what they ship
- [ ] Open a notes doc with the participant's name and date
- [ ] Have the product open in a separate tab but do not show it yet
- [ ] Decide: is this a problem discovery interview or a product evaluation interview?

**Problem discovery** = user has not used SecureDoc yet. Goal: understand their current pain.  
**Product evaluation** = user has used SecureDoc for at least 7 days. Goal: learn what worked, what broke.

Use this script for both. Sections are labeled accordingly.

## Opening (5 minutes)

Say this verbatim:

*"Thanks for making time. I want to be upfront about how this works: I'm going to ask you a lot of questions, and most of them don't have right or wrong answers. I'm trying to understand how you work, not evaluate you. If something I show you later feels confusing or broken, that's useful information — please say so. You can't hurt my feelings by being critical. Ready?"*

**Ask:**
1. Tell me about your work. What do you do, for whom, and what does a typical week look like?
2. What kind of documents are at the center of your work? *(Let them describe in their own words — don't suggest categories.)*

## Section 1: Problem Discovery (15 minutes)

*Use this section for ALL participants, including those who have used the product. It grounds the conversation in their reality before evaluating your solution.*

### Core questions (ask all of these)

3. Walk me through the last time you shared a document with someone outside your organization. What did you send, how did you send it, and what happened after?

4. After you sent it — did you know if they read it? How?

5. Has a document you sent ever ended up somewhere it wasn't supposed to be? *(Probe: forwarded to wrong person, shared with a competitor, distributed internally when you expected 1:1 sharing.)* What happened?

6. If I could show you, right now, exactly which pages of your last big deliverable the client spent the most time on — would that change how you prepared for the follow-up meeting?

7. What's your current system for sharing confidential documents? Walk me through it step by step.

8. Have you ever needed to "take back" a document after sharing it — revoke access, update a version, or stop someone from accessing it? How did you handle it?

### Persona-specific follow-ups

**If Architect:**
- Tell me about the last time you issued drawings to a contractor. What format, which tool, and what did you do when the design changed after issuing?
- Have you ever had a contractor present your work to a client as their own idea, or use your design details in a way you didn't intend?
- What's your current review workflow — how do structural engineers and clients mark up your documents?

**If Consultant:**
- You charge for your thinking. When a client shares your report with their board or with a third party — does that bother you? Has it happened?
- Do clients ever come to review meetings having clearly not read the report? How do you handle that?
- How do you know when a retainer client is actually engaged with your deliverables vs. just paying for them?

**If Professional Services Firm:**
- Who in your firm sends documents externally? Is it just principals, or does the whole team share work?
- Is there a standard way your firm handles confidential deliverables, or does everyone do their own thing?
- Have you ever had a compliance or liability issue related to a document being shared beyond the intended audience?

**If Startup Founder:**
- Walk me through your fundraising document workflow. How do you share your deck, data room, and financials?
- Have you used DocSend? What do you like about it, and what do you wish it did better?
- Have you ever had your deck forwarded to someone in your space who wasn't supposed to see it? What was the fallout?

## Section 3: Product Evaluation — First Impressions (10 minutes)

*Use ONLY for participants who have not used the product yet. Skip to Section 4 for existing users.*

**Share your screen or hand them the computer. Open the product.**

*Say: "I'm going to show you something we've been building. I want you to tell me what you think it is and who it's for — based on what you see, not what I tell you."*

12. Open the Upload Dashboard. Don't say anything. Wait 15–30 seconds.  
    Ask: *"What do you think this is? What would you do first?"*

13. Watch them interact (or ask them to narrate their instinct): *"Without clicking anything yet — if this were your tool, what's the first thing you'd want to do?"*

14. Show them the Share flow (Access Control → Create Link). Ask:  
    *"What do you understand about what this screen does?"*

15. Show them the Links tab with an active link that has views and analytics.  
    Ask: *"What's useful here? What's missing?"*

16. Show them the Feedback tab with an annotation from a "viewer."  
    Ask: *"If this came from your client, what would you do with it? Is this useful?"*

## Section 4: Product Evaluation — Return User Deep Dive (10 minutes)

*Use ONLY for participants who have been using SecureDoc for at least 7 days.*

**Ask them to share their own screen. Have them show you what they've done in the product.**

17. Walk me through what you've done in SecureDoc since you signed up. *(Listen for: what they actually used, what they skipped, what confused them.)*

18. What was the first thing you did that made you think "okay, this is useful"?

19. What was the first moment where you got stuck or confused?

20. Have you looked at the analytics for any of your links? What did you learn?

21. Has anyone left feedback or annotations on a document you shared? What was your reaction when you saw it?

22. What would make you recommend this to a colleague?

23. What would make you cancel?

---

## Section 5: Willingness to Pay (5 minutes)

*Ask this for all participants at the end. Keep it conversational — don't feel like a pricing survey.*

24. If this product did everything you needed it to — the document sharing, the tracking, the feedback — what would you expect to pay for it per month?  
    *(Do not anchor with a number first. Let them give a range. Accept silence — don't fill it.)*

25. At [$X] per month — [their number or $39 if they won't give one] — would this be in the category of "obvious yes," "I'd think about it," or "probably not"?

26. What would need to be true about the product for you to call it a "must-have" vs. a "nice-to-have"?

## Closing (5 minutes)

27. Is there anything important about how you work with documents that I haven't asked about?

28. Is there anyone else you know who has this problem? *(Ask for an introduction, not just a name.)*

*Say: "This was genuinely helpful. I'm going to share notes with you if you'd like to see what I heard. Do you want to stay close to this as we develop it?"*

## What Not to Do

- **Don't ask leading questions.** "Don't you think it's frustrating when clients don't read your reports?" gives you nothing.
- **Don't demo features unprompted.** If they don't discover a feature on their own, that's data.
- **Don't defend the product.** If they say "this is confusing," your response is "tell me more," not "actually it's designed to..."
- **Don't cut off silence.** Wait 5 full seconds after a question. The best answers come after the first pause.
- **Don't promise features.** If they ask for something that doesn't exist, say "interesting — we'll look into that" and note it. Not "we'll build that."
