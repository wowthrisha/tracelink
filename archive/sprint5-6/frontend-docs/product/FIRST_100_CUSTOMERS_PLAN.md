# First 100 Customers Plan — SecureDoc

**Date:** 2026-06-23  
**Sprint:** 5.0 — Product Positioning & GTM  
**Goal:** 100 paying customers (Solo plan or above). Not 100 signups. Not 100 free users. 100 people who paid.

---

## Why 100 Matters

100 paying customers proves willingness to pay across more than one segment. It generates enough revenue to fund the next hires, enough feedback to validate the ICP, and enough word-of-mouth to see organic acquisition emerge. It is the threshold between "this might work" and "this works."

At $39/month average, 100 customers = ~$3,900 MRR. At $79/month average (mix of Solo and Studio), it's ~$7,900 MRR. The goal is $5K+ MRR from 100 customers. That's the target.

## Phase 1: The First 10 (Days 1–30)

**Method:** Personal outreach only. No ads. No content. No cold email blasts. Founder's network.

### What to do:

**Day 1–5: Identify 30 candidates from your direct network**

These are people who:
- Have forwarded a document to someone they shouldn't have, and mentioned it
- Work in professional services and send reports or deliverables regularly
- Have complained about not knowing if a client read something
- Are currently fundraising or recently raised
- Asked "how do you share your work securely?"

Prioritize: consultants, architects, founders. Do not target engineers or enterprises yet — too long a sales cycle.

**Day 5–10: 1:1 conversations, not demos**

Don't show a demo deck. Show the actual product. Share your screen. Upload a document. Create a link. Show them the analytics. Ask: "Does this solve a problem you have?"

The question is not "would you pay for this?" It's "have you had this problem?" Then: "Would you try this for a month?"

**Day 10–30: Convert 10 paying customers**

These 10 do not need to be strangers. They can be former colleagues, clients, friends in consulting. What matters: they pay. Even $1 on a credit card is a signal. Don't give free months to avoid asking for money — you'll learn nothing.

**Success metric for Phase 1:** 10 paying users, at least 3 different use cases (e.g., consultant + founder + AE).

**Learning objective:** What's the first thing each customer did after signing up? What did they share? Did they come back in week 2? Why did the non-converters say no?

## Phase 2: Customers 11–40 (Days 31–90)

**Method:** Targeted community and content seeding. Still no ads. The goal is earned distribution.

### Channel 1: Startup / Founder Community (Target: 15 customers)

**Tactic:** ProductHunt launch.

A well-executed ProductHunt launch gets 1,000–5,000 unique visitors in 24 hours. The SecureDoc proposition ("DocSend but with DRM controls and viewer feedback") is immediately legible to this audience. 

ProductHunt launch requirements:
- A polished product page with 2–3 screenshots and a 60-second demo GIF
- A clear tagline: "Track who reads your documents. Control what they can do with them."
- A hunter with a following (or the founder themselves)
- "Ask HN: How do you share confidential documents?" post timed within 48h of launch

**Tactic:** YC community outreach.

YC Slack has a `#tools` and `#office-hours` channel. Founders actively fundraising are a high-intent audience. A message like: "We built a DocSend alternative that lets investors annotate your deck directly. Happy to give 3 months free to anyone in the fundraising process, looking for feedback" will get responses.

**Tactic:** VC firm investor relations departments.

IR teams at VCs that receive hundreds of decks per month have an incentive to recommend a tool that helps founders share professionally. Reach out to 10 VC associates with a partner link offer: "Any founder you introduce to SecureDoc gets 3 months free. We'll attribute the conversion to you." This creates a referral chain.

### Channel 2: Consultant Communities (Target: 10 customers)

**Tactic:** r/consulting (276K members). Post a case study:
"I built a tool that solves the 'client forwarded my report to a competitor' problem. Here's what happened." Authentic story, not a product pitch. Link in the comments.

**Tactic:** Indie Hackers. Build-in-public post. "I quit [job], spent 6 months building this, here's what I learned about document security for consultants." The IH community rewards transparency.

**Tactic:** Lenny's Newsletter community (Slack). Lenny's audience is product managers, operators, and consultants. A thoughtful message about the consultant document sharing problem will get engagement.

### Channel 3: Architecture Network (Target: 5 customers)

**Tactic:** LinkedIn targeted at "Principal, AIA, architecture firm." Write one post: "I sent drawing sets via email for 10 years. Then a contractor presented my design concept as their own idea in a client meeting. Here's what I do now." Ghost-write this for the founder or find an early architecture customer willing to share.

**Tactic:** Reach out directly to 20 principals at 5–30 person architecture firms in your city. "I'm a local founder, we built a tool specifically for sharing drawing sets. 30 minutes to show you?" In-person meetings convert architecture firms better than online channels — they're relationship buyers.

### Channel 4: Product-Led Viral (Target: 5 customers)

**How it works:** Every shared link created in SecureDoc is received by a non-user. That non-user sees a clean, professionally-designed viewer. At the bottom: "Powered by SecureDoc — secure document sharing." That link should be:
- A clean, small badge, not an interstitial
- Clickable, leading to the SecureDoc homepage
- Not shown if the user has disabled branding (Team plan feature)

**The viral coefficient:** If each paying customer sends 10 documents per month, and 5% of recipients click the badge, and 1% of those convert to a trial — at 40 customers that's 40 × 10 × 5% × 1% = 0.2 customers/month. Not significant yet. But as the network grows, this compounds. By customer 200, it's a meaningful acquisition channel.

**Success metric for Phase 2:** 40 paying customers. Mix: 15 founders/startup, 15 consultants, 5 architects, 5 miscellaneous.

**Learning objective:** Which channel converted at the lowest cost? What's the first-week behavior of activated vs. churned users?

## Phase 3: Customers 41–100 (Days 91–180)

**Method:** Systematize what worked in Phase 2. Add one paid channel. Build content.

### What should be working by Day 90

You should know:
- Which 1–2 personas convert fastest (hypothesis: founders + consultants)
- What the "aha moment" is (hypothesis: first time they see the per-page heatmap after someone views their document)
- The average time from signup to payment
- The most common reason for churn (hypothesis: "I don't share enough documents to justify it")

If these are not known by Day 90, pause Phase 3 and do 10 more customer interviews.

### Channel 5: SEO / Long-Tail Content (Target: 15 customers over 90 days)

**High-intent search terms to own:**
- "docsend alternative" — 3,000–5,000 searches/month, high commercial intent
- "how to share pdf securely with tracking" — educational, convertible
- "secure document sharing for consultants" — niche, low volume, high intent
- "investor deck tracking analytics" — founder audience
- "document sharing architecture firm" — vertical niche

**Content strategy:**
1. One comparison page: "SecureDoc vs. DocSend: What's different?" — targets brand-aware searchers
2. One use case page per segment: "Secure document sharing for architects," "Share investor decks with tracking"
3. One pain-point article: "Your 'view-only' Google Drive link isn't actually view-only. Here's why."

These pages will not rank for 60–90 days after publication. Start writing them in week 4.

### Channel 6: One Paid Channel (Target: 20 customers over 90 days)

**Recommended: LinkedIn Ads, not Google Ads.**

Why LinkedIn: The ICP (consultants, architects, engineers, founders) is reachable on LinkedIn with job title + company size targeting. Google Ads for "document security" will deliver enterprise IT buyers, not solo professionals.

**Campaign:**
- Target: "Independent Consultant," "Principal Architect," "Founder/CEO at 1–10 employees"
- Creative: A single testimonial from a real customer + the headline "Track which page stopped them."
- Budget: $500–$1,000/month. Expected CPL: $40–80. Expected conversion rate from trial: 15–20%. Net cost per customer: $200–$500.
- This is expensive per customer, but acceptable at this stage to validate paid acquisition economics.

If LinkedIn doesn't work at this budget, pause ads and return to community channels. Don't scale ads before finding a working creative.

### Channel 7: Partner Referrals (Target: 10 customers over 90 days)

**Partners to approach:**

1. **Business attorneys who review NDAs and IP agreements.** They understand the problem. A referral note: "I work with a tool that lets you share NDAs and confidential documents with automatic access controls. I recommend my clients use it."

2. **Fractional CFOs and business advisors.** They share financial models and reports. Their clients need secure sharing. A referral arrangement: 1 month free for each referral.

3. **Bookkeepers and accountants.** They share tax documents and financial statements externally. Often on G Suite, looking for better options.

**Partner acquisition method:** Reach 20–30 individuals directly. This is not a formal channel program — just 1:1 agreements with people who interact with the ICP daily and can recommend a tool.

**Success metric for Phase 3:** 100 paying customers. $5,000+ MRR.

## Churn Prevention: The First 30 Days

The biggest risk in the first 100 customers is activation failure — they signed up, never experienced the product's core value, and churned before month 2.

**Definition of activation:** Customer creates a link AND someone views that link AND the customer sees analytics showing which pages were viewed.

**If a user hasn't created their first link in 3 days:** Send one email. Subject: "Your first SecureDoc link takes 2 minutes." No marketing. Just: "Here's how to create your first link. If you get stuck, reply to this email and I'll do it with you on a call."

**If a user created a link but no one viewed it in 7 days:** Send: "Your link is live. Forward it to whoever you'd like to track. The analytics appear the moment they open it."

**If a user viewed analytics once and hasn't returned in 14 days:** Call them. Ask what they shared, who viewed it, and whether the information was useful. This is not a support call — it's product research.

**The goal is simple:** Every paying customer should experience the per-page heatmap at least once in their first 30 days. That is the retention feature. Everyone who sees it stays.
